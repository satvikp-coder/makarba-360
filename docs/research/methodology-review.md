# Methodology desk review — issue #4

Review scope: the planning versions of `methodology.md`, `sampling-plan.md`, `survey-questionnaire.md` and `consent-protocol.md` on the parent commit of this change. This is an engineering desk review, not an institutional decision or evidence of fieldwork. The user requested that separate project-internal approval not be treated as a prerequisite; no stakeholder approval is asserted here.

## Findings and treatment

| Area | Review finding | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Sampling | Purposive and convenience recruitment has unknown selection probabilities. The numeric targets are planning quantities, not a representative sample. | Report recruitment context, gaps, time periods and respondent bases; do not generalize percentages to the population or calculate probability-based margins of error. |
| Coverage | Varying routes and periods can improve coverage but cannot establish representativeness. Overlap between short and long interviews can double-count people. | Report coverage gaps and overlap explicitly; do not add interview counts as unique respondents without a validated linkage method. |
| Wording | Q01 assumes something is good; Q03's “biggest” asks for one ranked issue while some respondents may have none; Q04 asks about five years even when association is shorter. | In the next instrument revision offer “nothing”, “none”, “unsure” and refusal where appropriate; ask Q04 only when a respondent can answer, preserving “not enough association” and missing reasons. Review translated wording before use. |
| Consent | C02 refers to an as-yet unapproved termination policy, while the consent protocol distinguishes participation and optional publication scopes. | Resolve the permitted use and termination behavior in an approved, versioned script before collection; do not infer permission from a blank answer. |
| Location | The interview position and the place discussed are distinct observations. | Record each independently; an unavailable position is not a coordinate and cannot be replaced by a subject place. |
| Evidence | An account of change is a respondent's perception, even if the respondent claims official standing. | Keep source, attribution and uncertainty; require independent corroboration before recording a claim as a verified fact. Do not silently convert conflicting accounts into one factual assertion. |

## Independently reviewable examples

These examples are invented and contain no study data.

1. A respondent says “the road feels busier than it used to.” Expected: a respondent account of perceived change, with its question/version and consent context. Rejected: “traffic increased by 30%” or a verified traffic measurement; the answer provides neither a count nor corroboration.
2. A participant declines a condition rating. Expected: an explicit refusal or other applicable missing reason, excluded from the valid-rating denominator. Rejected: recording a zero or reporting it as a poor rating.
3. A researcher interviews at place A about place B. Expected: the interview position and subject reference remain independent. Rejected: replacing the missing interview position with B's geometry.

## Remaining limits

The instrument and consent script remain drafts; this review does not change their version or authorize recruitment. A verified study extent, institutional ethics and legal decision, appropriate language review, an approved withdrawal/contact process, and real-device and field evidence remain separate operational gates. No population finding, real boundary, research participant, or external approval is asserted.
